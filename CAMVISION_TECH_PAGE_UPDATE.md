### Post 2: Technical Value (Shows Deep Expertise)
> **Why default Auto-White-Balance (AWB) fails under indoor LED lighting (and how to fix it)**
>
> Many camera startups test their prototypes in the lab and find colors look fine under daylight, but the moment they bring the camera indoors under modern commercial LEDs, skin tones turn sickly green or purplish.
>
> Why?
> Most off-the-shelf sensor drivers rely on simplistic "Gray World" AWB assumptions. Modern phosphor-converted LEDs have sharp spectral spikes that fall off the Planckian blackbody locus. Without a calibrated bounding gamut or multi-illuminant weighting, the AWB algorithm gets confused and over-compensates.
>
> The fix:
> 1. Characterize the sensor's spectral response under specific test illuminants (D65, TL84, A, and modern 4000K LED).
> 2. Build dedicated chromaticity boundary polygons.
> 3. Implement smooth color temperature interpolation between illuminant presets.
>
> Need help calibrating your camera's color science and 3A algorithms? Drop us a message at CamVision Tech.

### Post 3: Case Study / Proof of Capability
> **Case Study: Fixing low-light motion blur on a smart security camera**
>
> A client reached out with a common problem: their 4MP battery-powered camera looked decent during the day, but at night under 2-lux lighting:
> 1. Any moving object (people, cars) turned into a smeared ghost.
> 2. Video bitrate skyrocketed, clogging network bandwidth.
>
> Here’s what we tuned in the ISP pipeline:
> - **Separated 2D Spatial vs 3D Temporal Noise Reduction**: Default tables had 3DNR temporal filtering set too aggressively, blending past frames onto moving objects (causing ghosting).
> - **Motion-Adaptive Filtering**: Configured motion detection weighting inside the ISP so static backgrounds received heavy temporal filtering (lowering bitrate by 24%), while moving areas used high-frequency spatial filtering.
> - **Local Tone Mapping (LTM)**: Flattened shadow noise amplification while preserving contrast in midtones.
>
> Result: Sharp, ghost-free night video ready for consumer shipment.
